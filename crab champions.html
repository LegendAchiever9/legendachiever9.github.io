Crab Champions

Played in 2023


Elevator Pitch

Crab Champions is a high-octane roguelite third-person shooter where you play as a gun-wielding crab, clear hordes of enemies, and build increasingly ridiculous combinations of weapons and modifiers. Its beach-party EDM soundtrack, absurd presentation, and crisp movement give the game immediate charm, but beneath the silliness is a compelling roguelite built around keeping up the pace.

Content

Crab Champions was created by Noisestorm, the artist behind the viral song "Crab Rave”, who transformed the crustacean conquest of the music charts into a wacky yet polished roguelite. The entire game embraces its own absurdity: you play as an armed crab who has to cut through waves of other crabs, the maps range from serene beaches to volcanic coves, and the entire soundtrack is composed of energetic EDM tracks that will make their way onto a gym playlist.



Underneath the goofy carapace though, is a well crafted shooter roguelite that knows the importance of keeping up the momentum throughout a run. These runs consist of clearing waves, choosing how to increase your power, collecting new weapons, and upgrading your build into a machine of mass maceration. Your starting loadout is a firearm, melee weapon, and grenade that can all be augmented by various modifiers that you’ll acquire throughout each run.

 

That sense of momentum is what ties the game's systems together. Crab Champions finds its stride when the systems accelerate together: your movement speed and power level grow to match the increasingly chaotic situations the game will throw at you. Crab Champions isn’t asking you to take it super seriously, you can just jump in with friends and shoot crabs while listening to excellent electronic music. That being said, mastering the satisfying movement and gunplay while experimenting with insane combinations provides plenty of mechanical depth to sustain dozens of hours of discovery and laughter.




Speaking of satisfying movement, let’s go over the fundamental movement mechanics that make the game so compelling. You can slide on the ground, air dash, and aim down sights (ADS). The air dash is effectively an aerial double jump with invincibility frames while ADS also places you into bullet time to facilitate more precise aiming and dodging. Individually all of these tools are useful, but together they form the backbone of what allows the game to reach breakneck pace.

 

By using slide, jump, then air dash you can build enormous speed to launch at high speeds to weave through enemy fire while using bullet time to land key shots. Maintaining your momentum while kiting around waves of enemies is a key element of Crab Champions that create a dynamic and exciting thrilling experience. When everything clicks, you're sliding, air-dashing, slowing down time to line up shots, and nearly flying around the arena while your increasingly absurd build deletes entire waves of crabs.



In any shooter I’d argue that gunplay, or the feel you get from using a weapon, is one of the most important aspects to get right. Crab Champions has an extremely responsive touch for its weapons that  gives you the reigns. The guns are tactile with strong feedback and satisfying sound effects which results in tight gameplay that combines exceptionally well with the rapid pace of moment to moment interactions. With twenty primary weapons that are all satisfying to use, Crab Champions earns its high-octane feeling before even needing the roguelite variation. The dynamic combat already provides a solid foundation that allows the roguelite systems to provide increasingly ridiculous ways to push the game further. 

 

While your loadout is an essential contributor to run diversity, the main systems at play in Crab Champions are mods and perks. These are item drops that you receive either after completing a round, activating a risk totem, or purchasing from a shop. There is an enormous quantity of these upgrades that range from weapon and grenade enhancements as well as changes to your core movement set. The important part isn't simply how many upgrades there are, but how naturally they can interact with one another. The way that different mods combine allows for your intuition to generally result in a powerful build. Stacking multiple of these augments that benefit the same component of your build will keep you ahead of the power curve while leaving plenty of room for experimentation or min-maxing depending on what you enjoy most.

 

The last primary mechanic is the portal you select after every round that determines the difficulty and reward of the following one. This isn’t anything new, a macro-game choice is quite common in the genre and this one doesn’t need to do something revolutionary to be effective. They provide a simple way to opt into higher risk, higher reward strategies for players who want to squeeze more out of a run. The most unique example of this is the parkour portal which challenges you to complete an obstacle course as fast as possible. 

 

The first runs you attempt in Crab Champions are all about exploring the possibilities. Does the minigun work apply multiple instances of elemental damage? Does the rocket launcher scale with critical strike chance? Can you make an entire build out of just grenades? Does the extra dash perk stack? 

 

Building up a devastating combo of modifications to max out your damage output is the main power fantasy for the game. You spend the early rounds amassing the ability to clear waves of enemies with ease and spectacle. Your build doesn’t need to be optimal to be fun — you’ll discover a multitude of different combos and wacky ways to bolster your crab that can all lead to victory. 

 

This is where the game’s progression starts to mirror its physical momentum. Early runs are about discovering what is even possible. Eventually you start recognizing stronger combinations and learning how to build around them, and the game shifts from experimentation toward mastery. Even if you find the killer combination for one weapon, testing the waters with the other different weapons and potential builds gives the game a great deal of replayability. 



If you find yourself matching the high pace of the game and wanting more of a challenge, you have access to that as well with challenges like nightmare mode and even ultra chaos for those who want to enter the cancrine matrix. This mode requires elite mastery over all movement techniques, leveling up all weapons, and high performance builds to conquer. I definitely recommend co-op if you’re aiming for this.



Speaking of co-op, Crab Champions allows you to play casually to relax and have fun with some incredibly overpowered and downright silly builds. However, it can also demand planning, optimization, and coordination to conquer the aforementioned toughest challenges. Crab Champions is already fun, and its silliness makes that fun especially easy to share with others. 



That flexibility is one of the game's greatest strengths. The same systems that let you mess around with an absurdly overpowered build with your friends can also become a serious test of execution when you turn up the difficulty. Regardless of what you’re looking for, you can certainly count on the game to provide an environment where you can push your mechanical prowess of dodging and aiming, find a build that lets you spray and pray, or just try to outspeed your friends in the parkour course.

Pain Points

Crab Champions shines brightest when a run starts accelerating. Its weaker moments are when you're stuck waiting for that acceleration to happen.

When you're flying around the map with a crazy build, the game delivers on its promise of high octane, hectic fun. When you're slowly working through familiar enemies with a build that hasn't found its identity yet, some of that magic disappears. A rough start into an unlucky build makes you feel the absence of that momentum and can take away from the game.



There is plenty of build diversity, but higher difficulties increasingly favor stronger and more optimized combinations. This can certainly pigeonhole players looking for the hardest content into a more restricted set of upgrades.

Runs also don’t have that much variance due to a relatively small pool of enemies and limited maps, but the crisp gameplay can make up for it. Especially when playing co-op, it's much more of a fun game to run with friends that doesn't need as much depth as others in the genre. You care a little less about seeing the same enemies again when you're a crab sprinting around an island with your friends while listening to great electronic music.

Music

The entire OST for Crab Champions was created by Noisestorm who took a break from publishing music under Monstercat to develop the game. Most tracks are either EDM, Drum and Base, with occasional forays into tech house and electro pop. The tracks as a whole are more on the repetitive side with relatively short runtimes and a single idea with not much variation. Given the volume of tracks and the volatility of the main game, this actually works out in the context of gameplay whereas it would be weaker as a standalone listen.



The soundtrack makes quieter moments feel funky and relaxing while encouraging high-intensity moments to be upbeat and exciting. Each track understands the importance of the game’s pace and uses that knowledge to amplify what you feel during gameplay - the movement is fast paced, increasingly insane numbers of enemies force you to move more, modifiers make you stronger and faster, and the soundtrack embraces this acceleration by encouraging you to keep going. Knowing when to pull back and when to floor it are crucial aspects of any score and Noisestorm absolutely delivered on that notion.




“Ace” is the song used for the first gameplay trailer and starts out with percussion instrumentation most commonly found in trap beneath a repeating light synth melody. The buildup takes a more distorted synth and the quarter note claps that are quite iconic for the genre. The beat drops at 0:45 with a truncated version of the earlier melody and a heavier bass to create a lilting feeling in the track which makes the repeated pattern more impactful. 

 

“Puffin Party” is the outro track for the trailer and also conjures a joyfully cute image from the title alone. We start out with retro synth pads which lead into on-the-beat claps yet again. The main melody starts playing on a high pitched synth at the back of the mix which is another classic trope in this style of music. The synth gradually fades in with a percussion buildup to drop at 0:30 where we actually have a harmony play underneath. The bridge brings the pads back and the piece overall has an extremely bright energy with a catchy tune — not much more you need for a signature track!




“Beach Funk” is one of the title screen tracks which showcases how the game pulls back on the soundtrack when outside of combat to ensure the hype moments land well. This is a tropical house song with a bass synth driving the track while steel drums pad out the chords. 0:31 brings us into a bridge where a flute plays a jazzy solo before getting doubled by a lower synth. 1:01 changes the shape a bit with the original bass line and parts of the solo taking a backseat in the mix while a warbled synth brings down the energy even further before looping back.

 

“Funky Beat” takes a similar approach while it plays throughout the safe zones after each round. A bright synth playing a syncopated melody and a heavily distorted bass synth create the groove before the full percussion section and harmony lines come in at 0:29. This continues until a truly funky keytar solo takes center stage at 0:42. Even with this solo, the piece carefully doesn’t outdoor itself in order to maintain these areas as a respite from hectic gameplay.




“Ice Cave” is one of the songs for combat rounds in the ice biome and is a tried and true drum and bass track. The main groove kicks in at 0:22 with a slower pace drum and bass melody and some hollow sounding synths to mimic the iciness of the level. 0:43 brings in a higher pitched synth to glide over  top and provide the energy necessary for the game’s combat while still keeping the theme of the level consistent.

 

“Desert Breaks” has the task of being a high pace combat track but also evoke the feeling of the desert biome. The piece starts out with a droning synth playing a syncopated pattern while a sitar styled synth plays fluttering notes in the background. At 0:14 those synths take the front of the mix while we add in a white noise fade in and cymbal hits to build up the drop at 0:29. This part of the song takes a whining synth with some metallic properties — while this melodic line isn’t fast, the percussion groove underneath keeps a strong beat to drive the piece forward and maintain the energy. At 0:58 we have a bridge section with the original fluttering synth and a deeply warbled bass act as the melodic pulse.




“Evil Song” plays during combat rounds on volcano islands and takes a departure from the usual, upbeat nature of the whole soundtrack; instead going for more of a dark synthwave vibe. Lower pitched, mechanical sounding synths and a pulsing bass set this track apart to emphasize the more serious nature of these levels. The main pattern still doesn’t have a lot of variance and primarily serves as a high tempo background to keep the engine running during the later stages.


“Claws Of Fury” as a boss theme takes a different approach to changing the pace. Staccato brass stabs kick off the song before the claps return along with the percussion section and deeper synths play out the bass. 0:28 brings in even more distortion with robotic sounds to create a cacophony of chaos into the drop. 0:43 takes us into a full tech house drop with heavily warped synths driving the melody, counter melody, and even quickly oscillating figures to keep the same pacing as the other late game stage tracks.

Summary

What started as an April Fools’ song turned into a fully fledged EDM concert disguised as a polished roguelite that truly understands how to make a run feel good. Crab Champions isn't trying to be deeply profound, it’s a game about being a crab with a gun, listening to electronic music, and becoming increasingly powerful until the screen can barely contain the consequences. The game isn’t afraid to be silly and the easy access of co-op is a great way to share that feeling with others.

Song of the Game

“Ace”
